# 10 — The Intendant

| | |
|---|---|
| Status | Accepted (2026-11-10) |
| Date | 2026-11-11 |
| Related decisions | ADR 0008, 0014, 0015, 0016; specs 05, 06 (§3, §5, §11), 07 (§01), 08, 09, 13 |

## 1. Purpose

The Intendant (infra inventory and mapping) keeps the Base camp: it surveys the ground, proposes the Map's entries and keeps the inventory of the agents and MCP servers in circulation on the cluster.

It never writes the Map. It proposes in its record's status, and a human turns the proposals into a pull request with `takecare review` (spec 13).

Out of scope for 0.3.0: the Bib (its own ADR and spec), cloud importers, any write.

## 2. Resources

- **`TakeCareIntendant`**, namespaced, one per Map scope. Its name is at most 44 characters, refused beyond at creation: its engine agent is `takecare-intendant-<name>`, and kagent names the agent's Service after it and sets it as a label value, both at most 63 characters. An Intendant stored with a longer name before the rule says so in `Ready` (reason `NameTooLong`) and renders nothing more; what an earlier release rendered for it (routes, model configuration) goes when it is deleted. It embeds `RoleCommonSpec` (level, scope, connectors, model, report) and adds:
  - `schedule`: a cron expression in UTC, daily by default (`0 3 * * *`); a schedule naming a time zone (`TZ=` or `CRON_TZ=`) is refused, as Kubernetes refuses it on a CronJob, and one stored before the rule sets `ScheduleValid` False (reason `ScheduleTimeZone`, one Warning event at the transition) and no survey starts;
  - `importers`: `Kubernetes` for 0.3.0, the default;
  - `connectors`: `cluster` and `knowledge` (at most 16 each), `map` (required, exactly one), `metrics` (optional, at most one) and `report`, each a connector name of at most 253 characters;
  - `report`: the report connector's destinations by level, as the Doc's.
- **`TakeCareSurvey`**, one record per survey, with a `RunStatus` (spec 06 §3).
  - Spec, immutable: `intendantRef`, `trigger` (`Schedule`, `OnDemand`, `MapRevision`, `Registration`; `OnDemand` by default) and `mapRevision`, the Map revision to survey against (empty: the one served when the survey starts).
  - Status: `layers[]` (per layer: seen, matched, proposed, not seen with the reason), `proposals[]` (§7), `inventory` (agents, MCP servers and tools on the cluster, with the first 100 items, §8), `blindSpots`.
  - Printer columns: phase, trigger, revision, proposals, published, age.
  - **Waiting.** A `Queued` survey that cannot start says why in `status.reason`, a waiting reason, never a failure. It is written only when it changes:
    - `IntendantBusy: the Intendant <name> runs survey <name>`: one survey runs at a time per Intendant. It clears when the survey starts.
    - `QueueFull: the survey queue is full (<n>)`: the leader's survey queue is full (its configured capacity). The survey is tried again every 30 seconds and clears when it starts. The Intendant gets one `QueueFull` Warning event, said again only once a survey of it has fit in the queue; the leader keeps that in memory, so a restart may say it once more.

    No survey is lost: a `Queued` survey is looked at every minute and put back in the queue if it left it, and a survey the runner could not read is put back.
  - **Writers.** Several writers meet on a survey's status: the queuer (`Queued`), the runner (`Running`, its outcome, the waiting reasons, a leader's resume of a `Running` survey back to `Queued`), the backstop (the end of a `Running` survey nobody holds, §5), the agent pass (its task) and the publication (`published`). Each writes on a fresh read under an optimistic lock and applies only to the phase it is for: an outcome is never written over an end, a task never after the outcome, and a waiting reason never on a survey that has started.

## 3. Triggers

- **Schedule**: the Intendant's `schedule`.
- **On demand**: a `TakeCareSurvey` created by hand, with the person's own rights; `takecare discover` (spec 13 §3.3) prints the command and never creates the survey itself. When the operator admits it, a survey without a controller reference gets one to its Intendant, set once and never moved: it then goes with its Intendant and is kept and deleted as the operator's own (§10, retention). Its Intendant is in its namespace, since the kind is namespaced and `intendantRef` names no namespace. A survey whose Intendant does not exist or is being deleted when it is admitted ends `Failed`, its reason naming the Intendant; it is never left `Queued`.
- **A new Map revision**: the `status.mapRevision` of the map connector changes. The connector reads it at each discovery, so a new revision reaches the Intendant up to one discovery interval (10 minutes) after the Map server loads it.
- **A registration**: a connector the Intendant binds, or a kagent agent of the engine namespace, appears or changes. Agents are watched in the engine namespace only, where the operator already reads them; other namespaces would need new rights.

A survey is due when the Intendant's observed state says so, read at each reconcile, never because of the event that woke it. Each survey the operator creates records, in its spec, what it observed: the Map's revision (`mapRevision`), the digest of the settled registrations (`registrations`), the registrations themselves (`registrationSet`) and why it was created (`reason`). The set names each entry so that a reader can find the object: its kind, its name with its namespace when that is not the Intendant's, and its generation (or `absent` for a bound connector that does not exist); it holds at most 200 entries and is not kept past that. What is due is compared with the newest of those surveys, the one `status.lastSurvey` names, whether it is in flight or ended and whatever its outcome. So a survey that ended `Failed` is not run again by the `MapRevision` or `Registration` rule until the state changes again or the schedule is due. Surveys made by hand record nothing and are not compared. Retention always keeps the newest ended survey. If an Intendant has only had surveys made by hand for a week, its last survey of its own may be swept, and the next reconcile then gives one `Registration` survey.

- **The Map's revision** is due when the map connector's revision differs from the newest survey's.
- **The registrations** are due when the digest of the settled registrations differs from the newest survey's and has held for the quiet period, 60 s. An install's registrations spread over about 40 s: on the e2e's cluster on 2026-11-19, the Intendant's agent appeared at 19:39:41, the doc-ops agent at 19:40:13 and the east connector at 19:40:22. So 60 s gives one survey where there were three. A `Registration` survey's reason says what changed since the newest survey's set: the entries removed, then added, then changed, each sorted ("agent kagent/takecare-doc-east removed; connector east added"), cut at the reason's 256 characters with how many more did not fit; past 200 entries it says how many changed and that the set is too large to list. `status.registrations` holds the digest seen now and `status.registrationsSince` when it was first seen. Both survive a restart, so a leader change does not restart the quiet period.
  - A bound connector counts once `status.observedGeneration` equals its generation.
  - An engine object counts once its status has observed its generation. For kagent 0.10.6's `Agent` that is `status.observedGeneration`; for its `RemoteMCPServer`, which has none, it is a condition's `observedGeneration`. An object whose status carries neither cannot tell, and counts as it is.
  - An object still changing is left out, so one that flaps restarts the quiet period at each change and gives no survey while it flaps, and so does a spec edited faster than the quiet period, for as long as it lasts; the schedule still runs.
- **An owed agent pass** is due when the newest survey's agent layer says its pass did not run because the agent was not ready, the agent is ready now and has been since after that survey was created, and it has stayed ready for the quiet period. The survey carries the `Registration` trigger, and its `spec.reason` says the pass was owed, so a reader does not look for a registration that did not happen. If that survey's pass is skipped again, the rule fires only after `EngineReady` has gone `False` and `True` again. An agent that flaps keeps moving the time it became ready, so the quiet period holds the survey.
- **The schedule** is due at `status.nextSurveyTime`.

Triggers are debounced: one survey in flight per Intendant, a survey created by hand included. The Intendant's reconciles are serialised, and just before creating a survey the operator reads the surveys live, not from its cache, so a survey the previous reconcile created is never missed. Triggers that fire together, or while a survey is in flight, give one survey, whose trigger is the first in the order `MapRevision`, `Registration`, `Schedule`. The one waiting is shown in `status.pendingTrigger`, and recomputed after a restart; a due schedule is kept there, since its time moves on. A suspended Intendant, or one not Ready, starts no survey: its due trigger is shown the same way, and the one pending survey runs when it is Ready again.

The Intendant's first survey starts when it is first Ready. When an agent is expected (a model and at least one tool), the first survey waits for the agent to be ready, at most 5 minutes after the Intendant became Ready. On that cluster, with images already pulled, the agent was Ready 32 s after the Intendant (19:39:42 and 19:40:13); pulling the images takes minutes. While it waits, `status.pendingTrigger` names the trigger, and the `EngineReady` message ends with "the first survey waits for the agent until <time>, then runs its import only". Past that time it runs with its import only, and its agent layer says why. The operator follows the Intendant's own agent by its observed generation and its `Ready` condition, so `EngineReady` changes when the agent changes, not at the next poll.

## 4. The deterministic import

Operator code, no model. It reads **through the `kubernetes` connectors** given to the Intendant, that is MCP behind the gateway (ADR 0008, 0016); the operator's own rights do not grow. It lives in one package, `internal/discovery`, which only the operator runs: `takecare discover` reads the newest survey and does not run the import (spec 13 §3.3; a local import is an open question there).

Layers, and how they map to spec 08:

- namespaces → `environment/<namespace>` (`environment/<cluster>.<namespace>` on a named cluster);
- Deployments, StatefulSets, DaemonSets and CronJobs → `compute`; their `app.kubernetes.io/part-of` or `app.kubernetes.io/name` label → a `service` proposal with `runsAs`;
- Services, Ingresses and HTTPRoutes → `network`; ServiceAccounts → `identity`; PersistentVolumeClaims → `storage`;
- the alerting rules Prometheus has loaded (PrometheusRules included), read through the `metrics` connector's rules tool (decided 2026-11-11) → `alertedBy` with the alert's expression (spec 08 §4). First by name: an entry whose `alertedBy` names a loaded alert without its expression, or with another, gets it. Then every alerting rule: one whose expression has an exact matcher naming one service (`service="…"`) or one workload (`deployment="…"` and the like) of the Map gives that entry the alert when it lacks it. Anything else proposes nothing; a name loaded twice with two expressions is not seen. Expressions are compared up to whitespace (Prometheus returns its own formatting), and the one proposed is Prometheus's. A `deployment` matcher names a Deployment, `statefulset` a StatefulSet, `daemonset` a DaemonSet, `cronjob` a CronJob; `workload` names any kind.

The import does not read the `knowledge` connectors: no standard MCP tool lists a knowledge server's records, so runbook entries come from the agent pass (§5) (decided 2026-11-11).

It calls only `namespaces_list` and `resources_list` on a cluster connector, and `get_rules` on the metrics connector, each when discovery classed it read and not content; the operator's MCP client refuses any other tool. `get_rules` is called for the alerting rules only, never the firing alerts, which carry label values, and never with another Prometheus or tenant: the client admits `type: alert`, `exclude_alerts: true`, alert names (`rule_name`) and rule group names (`rule_group`), nothing else. Two layers check the arguments: the gateway's route-level rule on the metrics route (§8) and this client check. The import lists objects and never fetches one, since an object's spec may carry values: of an object it keeps its kind, namespace, name and the two labels above, nothing else. The answers it parses are the add-ons' as pinned (`resources_list`'s structured content, `namespaces_list`'s table, `get_rules`' text, of which it keeps each rule's name and expression only); they are parsed strictly, and a shape it does not know is a `notSeen` line, never a guess. A `resources_list` answer whose items all lack the `Labels` key (a by-product of the server's table printer) is read, and a `notSeen` line in the service layer says that no service was proposed from that listing's labels; one where some items carry it and others do not is not recognized. `get_rules`' answer is read item by item: a rule's expression runs to the first `Duration:<n> Labels:` outside PromQL's string literals, so a matcher value in an expression holding that text, or `{Name:`, neither cuts the expression nor hides the layer. Known limits, not closed here, both because the pinned server (mcp-prometheus v0.11.11) prints a rule's label and annotation values unquoted: someone who can already write alerting rules on that Prometheus can make the import read a rule that does not exist, written whole in an annotation, which gives the import a name and an expression, nothing a real rule of theirs would not, the rules after it being still read; and a label or annotation value holding `{Name:` without a whole rule makes the answer unrecognized, a `notSeen` line on the alerting layer. Only a structured answer from the server can close them. The add-on cuts an answer at 50,000 characters and has no paging; when the every-rule answer is cut, the import reads again by name the rule groups it printed before the cut, eight a call and at most 128 groups (16 calls), once: an answer cut again is a `notSeen` line naming its groups, never read again. The groups after the cut cannot be named, and the layer says that the alerting rules after the last group read were not read; groups whose name the import may not ask for (below, §8), or past the 128, are counted in a `notSeen` line, never named.

Never Secrets, never ConfigMap values, never logs: content tools are not given to it.

Every discovered entry is `provenance: discovered`, `status: proposed`. An existing entry seen again is matched; it gets an update only when its sources or relations differ from what the survey sees. A survey never proposes a change of `lastSeen` alone: `lastSeen` is refreshed by an update made for another reason. A validated entry whose kubernetes sources were all surveyed (their kind listed in their namespace) and none seen keeps its `lastSeen` and gets a removal proposal; a source the survey could not list keeps the entry.

A ground's namespaces are the scope's, on every cluster its connectors read, and those of the kubernetes sources of the scope's entities in the Map, by the Doc's coverage rule (ADR 0014 §1-2, spec 08 §7); what nothing reads is a blind spot of the survey, with its reason.

The survey reads the Map through the Intendant's map route with `map_at` alone, the list of keys and then each entry at the same date, so that the whole index comes from one revision; N entries cost N+1 calls, four at a time.

**The import's deadline** is half the pass deadline (5 minutes by default), counted from the moment the runner takes the survey up. It cuts the import's calls; the Map's read and the metrics connector's canary before them run under the run's own context and only count against it (each is bounded: the Map's read stops at its first failed call, the canary is one call), so a slow one leaves the import less time and an overrun fails it at once. The inventory after the pass (§8) gets the same half of its own. The reason: the pass's deadline counts from the survey's start (§5), so an import that hangs on a server would otherwise eat the whole pass and leave the survey to the backstop; with the half, the agent keeps at least the other half, and a first run's worst case stays under the backstop's bound (§5); a survey resumed late runs its import again from its new take-up and can end past that bound, which is harmless because the backstop never ends a held survey (§5). It counts from the take-up, not from `status.startedAt`, because a resumed survey keeps its first start and runs its import again. Past it, the survey fails as an import failure does, `Failed` with the reason "the import did not finish within half the pass timeout"; the operator stopping is not that reason.

What a connector could not read is a `notSeen` line with its reason, never silence. An entry the import would propose is checked against the Map's schema (`mapserver.ValidateEntry`), as the agent's are: one that does not pass is not proposed, and its layer says so in a `notSeen` line naming the entry's key ("the import's proposal for this entry does not pass the Map's schema: not proposed").

### 4.1 Resource Graph importer

The import of a ground without Kubernetes, the item ADR 0021 left for later, is decided here for Azure. It lives in `internal/discovery/azure` and is **not wired into the survey yet**: no `importers` value names it and no cloud add-on serves its queries until a 1.x release. It is operator code, no model and no Azure SDK, reading through a querier the cloud add-on will supply, behind the gateway like every connector call (ADR 0008, 0021).

- **Request.** Resource Graph's REST API at version 2024-04-01, `POST /providers/Microsoft.ResourceGraph/resources`, with `subscriptions` always given and `options` `$top` 1000 (the documented maximum), `$skipToken` and `resultFormat: objectArray`. Never `managementGroups`, never the tenant scope: without subscriptions, Resource Graph reads everything the identity sees, delegations included. Whole subscriptions of a reach go together, at most 100 a request; a subscription narrowed to resource groups goes alone, its query narrowed to those groups. A group name that does not follow Azure's naming rule is not queried and is a `notSeen` line, and a subscription left with no group is not queried at all: the import never reads wider than the reach.
- **Two fixed queries, no free query anywhere.** Resource groups: `ResourceContainers`, type `microsoft.resources/subscriptions/resourcegroups`, projecting `id, name, subscriptionId, location`. Resources: `Resources` of the types in the table below, projecting `id, name, type, subscriptionId, resourceGroup, location`. Both `order by id asc`. Never `properties` nor `tags`, which carry settings: as the kubernetes import keeps kind, namespace and name, this one keeps ID, name, type, subscription and group.
- **Paging.** A `$skipToken` is followed with the same query and scopes, at most 50 pages (50,000 rows) a query. An answer with `resultTruncated` `"true"` and no token, past the 50 pages, unrecognized, or a failed call, is a `notSeen` line for each subscription of that request, on the environment layer for the groups query and on each resource layer for the resources query; such a scope is not read whole, so nothing in it is proposed for removal. Resource Graph answers only with what the identity may read, without saying the answer is partial, and refuses (403) only when it may read none of the subscriptions asked. So an empty answer proves nothing: a whole subscription whose groups query returns no group is a `notSeen` line ("no resource group visible in this subscription"), and so is a narrowed group the groups query does not return; neither is read whole. The answer is parsed strictly: `count` equal to the rows, `data` an object array, `resultTruncated` the documented string, and each row's `id`, `name`, `subscriptionId` (and `type`, `resourceGroup` for resources) strings. Throttling (`x-ms-user-quota-remaining`, `x-ms-user-quota-resets-after`, 429) is the transport's, within the import's deadline (§4).
- **The reach again.** Every row is checked against the reach of the connector that read it (ADR 0021 §6, case folded), and against its own shape: an ID whose subscription or group differs from its columns, a type outside the table, or a row outside the reach is dropped, and one `notSeen` line per connector and query counts them. A dropped row's ID still counts as seen: Resource Graph returned it, so no entry with that source is proposed for removal.
- **Mapping.**

  | Layer | Resource types |
  |---|---|
  | `environment` | resource groups, `environment/<group>`, every dot of the name turned to `-` |
  | `compute` | `microsoft.compute/virtualmachines`, `microsoft.compute/virtualmachinescalesets`, `microsoft.web/sites`, `microsoft.app/containerapps`, `microsoft.containerinstance/containergroups` |
  | `datastore` | `microsoft.sql/servers/databases`, `microsoft.dbforpostgresql/flexibleservers`, `microsoft.dbformysql/flexibleservers`, `microsoft.documentdb/databaseaccounts`, `microsoft.cache/redis` |
  | `storage` | `microsoft.storage/storageaccounts` |
  | `network` | `microsoft.network/loadbalancers`, `microsoft.network/applicationgateways`, `microsoft.network/dnszones`, `microsoft.cdn/profiles`, `microsoft.apimanagement/service` |
  | `identity` | `microsoft.managedidentity/userassignedidentities` |

  Disks, network interfaces, security groups and virtual networks are left out as noise; managed Kubernetes clusters are grounds read through their own front (ADR 0016). Each entry's source is `azure: <ARM ID>` as returned. Compute, datastore and network entries are `deployedIn` their group's environment entry (spec 08 §2). No service is proposed: Azure has no label convention like `app.kubernetes.io/part-of`, and tags are content.
- **Keys.** An existing entry is matched first, by its `azure` source with case folded, so a validated entry keeps its key; a matched entry gets an update only when it lacks the `deployedIn` the survey sees. A new key is `<layer>/<name>`, the name lower-cased and every character the key pattern refuses turned to `-`. When names collide within the survey, or with a key the Map already holds, each colliding key is qualified by its resource group when that tells them apart, else by the first 8 hex characters of its subscription, else by both (`compute/api-rg-shop`, `compute/vm-web-1-11111111`); an environment is qualified by its subscription only. An environment's key never carries a dot, qualifier included: `environment/<cluster>.<namespace>` names a namespace (ADR 0016 §5, spec 08 §2), and a resource group name may hold one. A key still taken after qualifying, by the Map, by another proposal of the survey, or by another row of the same name (two databases `orders` on two servers of one group), is proposed for none of the rows that share it, and the layer says so in a `notSeen` line per row naming its ID. Every proposal passes `mapserver.ValidateEntry`, as in §4.
- **Removals.** A validated entry of these layers whose sources are all `azure`, each in a scope read whole and of a type the import lists, and none seen or dropped, gets a removal proposal with its `lastSeen` kept, its evidence naming the connector and the table. A scope is read whole by a connector when that connector's own groups query read every row and showed the identity can read it: at least one group of a whole subscription, the group itself of a narrowed one; for a resource, the same connector's resources query read every row too. A resource row, or another connector's answer, never makes a scope read whole, and the evidence names the connector that read it. A whole subscription is read with rights on the whole subscription: rights on some of its groups only make the others look gone. An entry with another kind of source, or a source the survey did not read whole, is kept.
- **Fixtures.** No Azure ground is read in the repository's tests: they run on synthetic answers written from the documented 2024-04-01 shape, named as such (`internal/discovery/azure/testdata`).

## 5. The agent pass

After the import, one kagent run, traced `invoke_agent intendant` with the same contract as the Doc's, receives the import's result and proposes what the import cannot see:

- `dependsOn`, from the hostnames in environment variables and the names of connection settings;
- `belongsTo`, from labels and annotations;
- `documentedBy`, from the Topo;
- `runbook` entries with a `knowledge` source (spec 08 §3), from the records the knowledge tools return, with `documentedBy` on the entries they apply to.

Each proposal carries its evidence: the tool calls that support it.

The agent reads workload specs with the cluster's read tools, so environment values reach the model through the gateway, under the Ranger's Markers (spec 09). A proposal and its evidence carry names and hostnames, never a value; evidence names the tool calls, it never quotes their output.

Tools: the Map's five, the cluster's `read` tools and the knowledge tools; no content tool unless it is listed. A listed content tool that no connector given to the agent exposes is named in `ConnectorsReady`'s message and in one `ContentToolUnknown` warning, as the Doc says it (spec 02): said again only when the set changes, never at every reconcile. The agent's proposals join the import's, with `provenance: proposed`. The instructions forbid asking a question (kagent gives every agent `ask_user`): no one answers during a survey.

The pass runs after the import when the Intendant's agent is ready (`EngineReady`):

- **Before it:** a survey that has not started waits `Queued`, and its reason says why (§2, Waiting).
- **Its deadline** is 10 minutes counted from the survey's start (`status.startedAt`), the engine getting its usual share. A survey resumed after a restart keeps that start and follows its recorded task (`status.engine.taskID`); a new pass counts from its own start.
- **A survey whose end was never written** (an outcome whose status write failed) stays `Running` only until its bound: its start (`status.startedAt`, its creation before it started) plus twice the pass deadline, one scrape interval and 40 seconds. The factor 2 covers a run's worst case, one and a half pass deadlines and the scrape wait: the import within the pass's time (§4), the pass to its deadline from the start, the scrape wait, then the inventory's half; the 40 seconds are the cancellation's bound and a margin for the status writes. The bound applies to a survey nobody holds and is not a deadline for a held one: the runner's own survey is never touched, so the factor only delays the cleanup after a crash. Past it, the operator ends the survey `Failed` with reason `Abandoned` ("the run's end was not recorded" and when), `UsageAttributed` False (`NotMeasured`) with the same words, and one `Abandoned` Warning, as the Doc's backstop does (spec 02 §5). A new leader holds every survey until its resume has put back those left `Running`, which it then runs again; a resume that cannot list them is not retried, and the bound ends them.
- **A task stopped** waiting for an input or an authorization ends the pass at once and is canceled.
- **The agent is not re-rendered during a pass.** kagent 0.10.6 rolls the agent's Deployment on any change of its rendering (the pod template's `kagent.dev/config-hash`, RollingUpdate with `maxSurge` 1 and `maxUnavailable` 0), and the old pod takes the pass's task with it. So while a survey of the Intendant is `Running`, a new rendering waits, at most until that survey's pass deadline; the last rendering asked for is applied when the survey ends or the deadline passes. `EngineReady` stays True with reason `RenderPending`, naming the survey and the deadline, and `status.renderPendingSince` says since when. The gateway's routes and policies are never deferred. A `Queued` survey does not hold the rendering: it starts on the new agent, once it is ready. The Doc does the same for its investigations (spec 02).
- **A pass that does not finish**, fails, or answers what cannot be read leaves the import's proposals: the survey is Completed, and its `agent` layer says why in a fixed phrase. A pass that asked a question anyway (`input-required`) says so ("the agent pass asked a question, which no one answers during a survey; the question is not kept"). The model's text is never kept in the status, a log or a span.
- **A pass its deadline ended:** the agent layer's line is "the agent pass did not finish: " followed by what the follow saw, as for the Doc's runs (spec 02 §2): the last reads' failures by status or class, the last read that found the task working, or a pause of the follow; never a text of the engine's.
- **Usage:** the pass's model calls and tokens are measured on the Intendant's own model route, as the Doc's are (spec 02 §5), and recorded in the survey's `status.usage`. One survey runs at a time per Intendant, so it is exact, with two exceptions. A pass resumed after a restart lost its start and records no usage. Either way the survey's condition `UsageAttributed` says it: `True` (`Measured`), or `False` (`NotMeasured`) with why, as the Doc's records do (spec 02 §5). And the pass after one that did not finish is marked approximate: kagent refuses cancellation (below), so the earlier pass may still call the model on the same route; that is one of the corrections after 0.3.0.
- **kagent's record:** kagent keeps the pass as a session in its database, with its events (prompts, tool calls and results, answers) and its task; `captureContent` does not govern it. The chart sets kagent's `sessionRetentionDays` to 30: a session idle for 30 days (counted from its last write) is deleted with its events and tasks by kagent's daily cleanup, for every agent of that kagent.
- **Cancellation:** kagent 0.10.6's runtime refuses it, so a pass past its deadline may run on in its pod and call the model. kagent's Agent has no turn limit at this version; the runtime's own limit is 500 model calls a run. The Ranger's per-model cap on the Intendant's model route (spec 09 §4) is what bounds it: set one.

## 6. Levels

- **Apprentice**: the survey and its proposals in status. Nothing is published; the proposals stay in the status (or go to its `review` destination when one is set, as for the Doc, spec 02).
- **Practitioner**: the same, plus a digest that the operator publishes to the Intendant's report destination, so that a team reviews the proposals (decided 2026-11-10). The digest holds counts and keys, never content. It is one message per Completed survey whose proposals changed since the Intendant's last posted digest (decided 2026-11-13), posted through the report connector as the Doc's diagnoses are: the counts by action and by source, the first 20 keys in the status's order and how many more, the survey's name, its Map revision and its ConfigMap. Its outcome is in the survey's `status.published`. It is posted after the survey ends, so its retries never hold the next survey. It carries the survey's identity as the idempotency key (spec 05, "Publishing once"): a retry, or an operator stopped between the post and its record, does not post it again while the report server remembers the key (an hour); past that, or after a restart of the report server, it is posted at least once. After a restart of the operator, the digests left unposted go one at a time, oldest first, one second apart (Slack's pace for one channel). The digest's context says how many parts of the ground were not read, by layer, and the command that shows which and why; its header names the Intendant unless its name is `intendant`. Every `mrkdwn` text is `verbatim`, so that Slack links nothing it was not given as markup. A survey that Failed or was Dropped posts nothing.
- **Expert**: not implemented in 0.3.0; `LevelSupported` says so.

"Changed" compares a fingerprint, a hash of the ids of the full set and their total, with the Intendant's `status.lastDigest`. That record is written only after a successful post, with the survey, the connector and the destination. A survey with the same fingerprint, to the same connector and destination, posts nothing, and its `status.published` names the survey whose digest it repeats. Another destination or connector posts again, and so does the survey after a failed post, since the failure records nothing. A survey with no proposal posts nothing, and in 0.3.0 no "all clear" message tells a team that everything was merged. `docs/how-to/review-proposals.md` says how to review the proposals by hand.

Invariant 4 (spec 06 §5) holds: there is no `propose` tool, and `takecare review` (spec 13 §3.4) is a human's act: it writes the accepted proposals as Map files and commits them on a local branch of the person's checkout, never in the cluster and never pushed.

## 7. Proposals

Each item of `proposals[]` is `{id, action, key, entry, evidence[], source}`:

- `action`: `Add`, `Update` or `Remove`;
- `entry`: the YAML that the file of spec 08 §4 would hold;
- `source`: `Import` or `Agent`.

Ids are stable across surveys (a hash of the action and the key), so that `takecare review` can tick what was taken (spec 13 §5, `.takecare/review.yaml`).

The status is bounded in size: it keeps the counts and the first 20 proposals (20 entries of at most 8 KiB, 160 KiB at worst). The full set is in a ConfigMap owned by the survey, which never holds content:

- it is `survey-<survey>-proposals` in the operator's namespace, named in `status.proposalsConfigMap`;
- its data is one key per proposal id, the proposal as YAML in the status's shape, in the import's order;
- its data is bounded at 900 KiB; what does not fit is left out, and the layer of the first proposal left out says so first among its `notSeen` lines: "the full set exceeds the ConfigMap's size: N proposals left out";
- a survey run again replaces it (deleted, then created); a ConfigMap of that name the survey does not own is never touched, and the survey fails with "the proposals could not be recorded".

The operator's rights on ConfigMaps in its own namespace are create, get and delete: no list, no watch, no update. It reads them through the API reader, not the cache. Kubernetes cannot narrow these rights to the surveys' ConfigMaps (a Role's `resourceNames` cannot scope `create`, and the names are per survey), so the right covers every ConfigMap of the release namespace: the operator may read, create and delete any of them. The code reads only a survey's own ConfigMap, and deletes one only when the survey owns it, with a precondition on its UID. This residual right is kept: the proposals stay in ConfigMaps of the release namespace, and the chart's README states the right in its security requirements.

## 8. The inventory and the Intendant's routes

The survey's inventory is the cluster's: the agents and MCP servers in circulation there, in `status.inventory` and an `inventory` layer that only counts (nothing is matched or proposed). It has four parts, each read in one place:

- the kagent `Agent`s of the engine namespace, from the operator's own cache (it already watches them for registrations), with the tools each is given. These are the operator's own objects or the engine's, not the customer's ground, so discovery through the connectors (§4) is not bent;
- the `TakeCareConnector`s, from the operator's cache, with their discovered tools;
- the kagent `Agent`s in the Intendant's scope, through the `kubernetes` connectors only, when the add-on has the opt-in read rule on `kagent.dev` agents (`addons.kubernetes.extraResources`, off by default; get, list and watch). Otherwise a not-seen line says so: "agents in scope cannot be listed: the kubernetes add-on has no kagent.dev rule". The operator's own client never reads a customer namespace;
- the kagent `SandboxAgent`s in the Intendant's scope (kagent v0.10.6, `kagent.dev/v1alpha2`), the same way, when the add-on has the separate opt-in read rule on `kagent.dev` sandboxagents (`{group: kagent.dev, resource: sandboxagents}` in `extraResources`, off by default; get, list and watch). The `agents` opt-in does not imply it. Otherwise a not-seen line says so: "sandbox agents in scope cannot be listed: the kubernetes add-on has no kagent.dev sandboxagents rule". A SandboxAgent is an item of kind `Agent`, the inventory's kinds being `Agent` and `Connector`. **Limit**: an `Agent` and a `SandboxAgent` of the same name in one namespace make one item.

`status.inventory` counts all of them and lists the first 100, sorted by kind, namespace and name. Its `tools` count is the connectors' discovered tools, the tools in circulation; an agent's given tools are among them, so they are not counted again, and each item keeps its own. An agent listed through the kubernetes connector shows 0 tools: its listing does not say.

No agent registry is installed in 0.3.0. Where an agent's equipment lives is ADR 0019: a Bag is packed from Git at configuration time. agentregistry was assessed on 2026-11-13 at v0.4.0. It is not installed because it does not offer what ADR 0019 needs (an approval state on its entries, an install without rights on the cluster, an engine that consumes it). It is watched, and taking it up would be a new ADR.

The Intendant's routes on the gateway (to its map, cluster and metrics connectors) identify it by their path alone, as the Doc's do (ADR 0008): any pod the gateway admits, the engine's included, could call them, and would get only their allow-list, the listing tools and `get_rules`; the Bib (spec 09) closes this. Each route's allow-list carries the operator's tools and the agent's (§5): the Map's read tools, the cluster's read tools without content and the knowledge connectors' read tools. `get_rules` is the operator's and is never given to the agent. A slot gives the agent the read tools of the connector named in it, whatever their type (tools carry no type); a content tool still needs `spec.contentTools`. A connector named in several slots (spec 05 gives a connector several types) has one route: its allow-list is the union of what each slot allows, the agent is given the union of each slot's read tools without `get_rules`, and the argument rule below is kept while any of its slots is the metrics one. While the route is kept, the operator writes that rule before an allow-list that admits `get_rules`, and deletes it only after the allow-list stops admitting it.

The gateway checks tool names in its MCP authorization (`backend.mcp.authorization`), and `get_rules`' arguments in a route-level authorization of its own on the metrics route (`traffic.authorization`, action `Require`, a policy `<route>-calls`). In agentgateway v1.6.0, MCP authorization rules never see a call's arguments (`mcp.tool.arguments` is not set there), while a route-level rule reads them on `tools/call`; list requests have no `mcp.tool`, so the rule lets them through and the tool stays listed ([agentgateway 1.6.x, CEL variables](https://agentgateway.dev/docs/kubernetes/1.6.x/reference/cel/variables.md)). The route is a path prefix, and the gateway sets `mcp` only on a POST it reads as an MCP JSON-RPC request (not on client registration, for one), so the rule is rendered behind `(request.method in ["GET", "DELETE"] || has(mcp.methodName))`: a request the gateway does not read as MCP is refused on every path below the route, except a session's stream (GET) and close (DELETE), which carry no call. So `get_rules`' arguments have two layers, the route's rule and the operator's client check (§4), as ADR 0008 asks. The rule admits `get_rules` with `type` `alert`, `exclude_alerts` true and no key but these and `rule_name` and `rule_group`; `rule_name`, when given, is a list of 1 to 100 alert names (a letter, then letters, digits and `_`); `rule_group`, when given, is a list of 1 to 8 group names, each 1 to 128 characters of letters, digits, `_`, `.`, `:` and `-`, starting with a letter or a digit. No accepted group name holds a space, a slash, a quote or a brace, so `rule_group` cannot carry a URL, a PromQL expression or another argument, and `prometheus_url`, `org_id` and `file` stay refused because the key list is closed. A value of another type (a string, an object, a list holding a number) makes the expression an error, which the gateway answers with a refusal. Its text:

```text
!has(mcp.tool) || mcp.tool.name != "get_rules" || (mcp.tool.arguments.type == "alert" && mcp.tool.arguments.exclude_alerts == true && mcp.tool.arguments.all(k, k in ["type", "exclude_alerts", "rule_name", "rule_group"]) && (!has(mcp.tool.arguments.rule_name) || (size(mcp.tool.arguments.rule_name) > 0 && size(mcp.tool.arguments.rule_name) <= 100 && mcp.tool.arguments.rule_name[0] == mcp.tool.arguments.rule_name[0] && mcp.tool.arguments.rule_name.all(n, n.matches("^[A-Za-z][A-Za-z0-9_]*$")))) && (!has(mcp.tool.arguments.rule_group) || (size(mcp.tool.arguments.rule_group) > 0 && size(mcp.tool.arguments.rule_group) <= 8 && mcp.tool.arguments.rule_group[0] == mcp.tool.arguments.rule_group[0] && mcp.tool.arguments.rule_group.all(g, g.matches("^[A-Za-z0-9][A-Za-z0-9_.:-]{0,127}$")))))
```

`x[0] == x[0]` holds on a non-empty list only, so an object, whose keys `size` and `all` would accept, is refused; agentgateway v1.6.0 lists no `type()` function. The operator's client check, this rule (evaluated by cel-go in a unit test) and the chart smoke (on the pinned gateway) are tested against one table of cases, `internal/mcp/testdata/rules-args.json`, the canary's call among them.

A call the rule refuses gets an HTTP 403. Until the gateway has both Accepted and Attached the rule, and the survey has seen it refuse a canary, the metrics connector counts as not usable. The status alone does not prove enforcement: agentgateway can drop a field, or keep its last good configuration, while both conditions stay True. So at each survey, before the alerting layer, the operator sends one call on the Intendant's metrics route, `get_rules` with the survey's arguments and one extra key, `takecare_canary`, which the rule refuses, and requires the gateway's 403. A gateway that refuses everything also refuses the canary; the survey's own call then fails and the layer says so. Let through, the rule is not enforced; a timeout or a server error leaves it unproven.

The route stops admitting `get_rules` by name (its MCP authorization lists no tool) while the status refuses the rule, and from a survey whose canary was not refused until a survey's canary is refused again; a new Intendant's route admits it once the status says enforced, and its first survey proves the rule before it reads. The gateway checks the route rule before the tool list, and a Gateway-level traffic policy cannot drop the tool list (frontend and backend policies never inherit), so the canary still proves the rule on a closed route: refused with 403 by the rule, it is proven; refused with 400 and JSON-RPC `-32602` by the tool list, it is not. The survey that proves it on a closed route does not read the rules; the route admits `get_rules` again for the next survey, which reads them.

Whenever the survey does not call `get_rules`, its alerting layer says why in a fixed phrase: "the gateway does not enforce the argument rule on the metrics route" when the status says so, "the gateway let through a call its argument rule on the metrics route refuses" when the canary was let through, "the gateway's argument rule on the metrics route is not enforced; the route's tool list refused the canary" when the tool list refused it, "the gateway's argument rule on the metrics route could not be checked" when it was unproven, and, without a problem, "the gateway's argument rule on the metrics route is proven: the rules are read from the next survey". `ConnectorsReady` names the problem (reason `ConnectorsDegraded`), from the status or from the last completed survey, while the Intendant stays Ready; the Intendant gets a `MetricsRouteClosed` Warning event when a survey's canary closes the route, and a `MetricsRouteReopened` event when a proof reopens it. The canary's answer is dropped unread; it is not a survey call, and it is counted neither in the layers nor in the usage.

Between a change that drops the rule and the next survey's canary, the metrics route still admits `get_rules` by name: the Intendant's routes identify it by path only (above), so a caller the gateway admits could call `get_rules` with any arguments, and read the alerting rules and, without `exclude_alerts`, the firing alerts. From that survey until a later one proves the rule again, it does not. The Intendant's agent is given no metrics tool (§5), so its pass runs either way. The prometheus add-on's egress NetworkPolicy also keeps a caller's `prometheus_url` from reaching anything but its Prometheus. These rules are tested again on the chart smoke at each agentgateway version.

## 10. Telemetry

One trace per survey (ADR 0006), its ID in the survey's `status.traceID` and in the operator's log when the survey fails. Its root is `invoke_agent intendant`, an error span with the survey's fixed reason when it fails. Under the root:

- `read map`, the Map's read at one revision, with the number of entries;
- one `list <connector>` per ground, timed as the listing ran, with its cluster and number of namespaces;
- `read rules`, the alerting rules' read, when a metrics connector is bound;
- one `layer <name>` per layer, carrying its counts: seen, matched, proposed, not seen;
- `agent pass`, the agent pass (§5): its task, outcome and attempt (`takecare.run.*`), an error with its fixed phrase when it does not finish. The root then carries the agent's identity, the survey as the conversation, and the platform model. kagent's own spans for the pass carry kagent's context id as `gen_ai.conversation.id`, not the survey's, and each pass is a new kagent session: the operator's trace and kagent's are joined by the pass's task id (`takecare.run.*`), not by the conversation id.

The attribute names (`takecare.survey.*`, `takecare.run.*`) live in `internal/telemetry/semconv` like every other.

Metrics are gauges, not counters: the same proposals come back at every survey until a human merges them, so a total would count one proposal many times. Each Intendant's last completed survey sets them, and its series go when the Intendant goes:

- `takecare_survey_proposals{intendant,action,source}`: what awaits review now;
- `takecare_survey_not_seen{intendant,layer}`: what the survey could not read.

One counter, since a survey ends once:

- `takecare_surveys_total{result}` (completed, failed, abandoned): surveys ended, counted once. The writer whose patch ended the survey counts it: the runner (completed, failed), a survey failed at its admission (failed), the backstop (abandoned).

The `intendant` label has a few values per cluster.

Retention: an ended survey (Completed, Failed or Dropped) older than seven days is deleted, every hour, by the leader; each Intendant keeps its newest ended survey however old it is, so that its last outcome stays readable, and its newest Completed one, which decides its metrics route (§8).

## 11. Acceptance criteria

1. On the demo, a survey proposes the demo Map's compute, network, identity and environment entries, and finds no difference with the validated demo Map other than the declared ones.
2. On the demo, `HighErrorRate`, which `service/billing-api` declares, gets the expression Prometheus loaded; a rule naming exactly one service or workload of the Map lands as `alertedBy`, with its expression, on that entry.
3. `RB-014` in the corpus lands as a `knowledge` source on a runbook entry that documents `service/billing-api` (`documentedBy`), whatever key the agent gives that entry.
4. A Deployment deleted from the scenery gives a removal proposal; its entry keeps `lastSeen`.
5. A namespace no connector reads is a blind spot of the survey, with its reason, not an omission.
6. The read-only guard test is unchanged and green; the add-ons' RBAC is unchanged; the operator gains only create, get, list, watch and delete on `takecaresurveys`, patch on its status, and create, get and delete on ConfigMaps in its own namespace (§7).
7. The survey's inventory lists the cluster's agents and connectors with their tools; the agents in the Intendant's scope are listed when the kubernetes add-on may read them, and a not-seen line says so otherwise.
8. A new Map revision triggers one survey; two triggers during a run give one more survey, not two (proven in envtest).
9. A scored bench of the Intendant on a real model, its agent's proposals compared with a hand-written expected set (`make demo-bench-intendant`, `test/bench/README.md`). It surveys the demo against the demo Map without `service/billing-api`'s `documentedBy`. Its bar at a release is five runs ok out of five (`BENCH_MIN=5`, docs/release.md), set by the product owner after the first report, 5/5 on 2026-11-12.
